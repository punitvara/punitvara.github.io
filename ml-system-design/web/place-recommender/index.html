How good is this list, really?

Bars: hidden true satisfaction of each suggested place for this person. Dashed line: the best achievable average among the candidates.
What to notice. Switch the model from Popularity to Full ranker and watch the pins move toward you and the satisfaction bars rise. Drag the time slider from 9:00 to 22:00: cafes and bakeries give way to bars. Raise Diversity and the list covers more categories at a small cost in score. Turn on Explore and places with a yellow ring enter the list; without that they never collect data. Coarsen location precision and the results degrade, which is the privacy trade-off in one slider.

2. Offline evaluation: which model is better?

Trained on days 0-71, tested on check-ins from days 72-89. Recall@10: was the place the person went to in the top 10?

What to notice. Global popularity finds 10% of visits. Restricting to nearby places more than doubles it, before any learning. The taste embedding alone is no better than nearby popularity; adding distance and then context is what gets to 47%. Change the slice to "new place": every model drops, and retrieval alone cannot fix it.

What the full ranker relies on

What to notice. Distance and "is this category popular at this hour" outweigh the personal taste score. For local recommendations, where and when beat who. Say this when asked which features matter most.

3. Pitfalls

Distance bias: "not visited" mostly means "too far"

What to notice. Four in five check-ins are within 2 km. If negatives are sampled from the whole city, the easiest way to separate visited from unvisited is to learn where each person lives, so the score tracks distance from home (0.45) and barely tracks taste (0.08). It then fails when the person travels. Sampling negatives near the visited place forces the embedding to learn taste, and distance goes in as an explicit feature.

Position bias in click logs

What to notice. The old ranker sorted by popularity, so good but obscure places sat in slots 8 to 10 where few people look. Their raw click rate is about 10% when their true appeal is near 90%. Showing a small share of traffic in random order measures how attention falls with position; dividing each click by that factor recovers the truth. Set random traffic to 1% and the correction gets noisy.

Popularity feedback loop

What to notice. Using raw visit counts as a feature rewards places for having been shown. The head takes about 80% of impressions and only a third of the catalogue is ever seen. Replacing counts with position-corrected rates and adding an uncertainty bonus shows over 90% of the catalogue, and people end up clicking places they like more.
