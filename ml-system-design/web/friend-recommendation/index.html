Interview guide

A 1,000-user synthetic social network with communities, schools, employers and uploaded contact books. The system sees the graph as of "today" and must predict friendships that form later. Those future friendships are hidden from the model and used only to score it.

1. The system at a glance

Recall by stage (top 10 shown to the user)

What to notice. No single generator finds more than ~58% of future friends, but their union finds ~83%. Retrieval sets the ceiling; the ranker decides how much of it survives the cut to 10. Say "multiple candidate generators, measured by recall" before you say anything about the ranker.

What the ranker learned (permutation importance)

What to notice. Mutual-friend count is the feature everyone names first, yet the model leans on contact-book overlap, embedding similarity and profile views. Counts of mutual friends are mostly redundant once Adamic-Adar and Jaccard are present.

3. Retrieval at 1B users: the ANN trade-off

Embeddings are clustered into 32 cells (an IVF index). A query scans only the nearest cells.

What to notice. Scanning 12% of the vectors keeps 97% of the exact top-20. At a billion users that is the difference between an infeasible scan and a few milliseconds. The knob (cells probed) trades recall for latency, and you tune it against downstream top-K recall, not against exact-search recall alone.

4. Pitfall: label leakage from a random split

Train the same model two ways and compare the offline score with what happens in production.

What to notice. With a random split the features are read from today's tables, after the friendship already exists. Profile views between two people jump once they are friends, so the model learns a consequence of the label. Offline AUC looks near perfect and production AUC collapses. The fix is point-in-time features: snapshot at T, label from the window after T.

5. Pitfall: hub bias and the feedback loop

Simulate several rounds of serving friend suggestions to everyone, where accepted suggestions become new edges that feed the next round.

What to notice. Hubs are 5.7% of users but take about twice that share of slots without mitigation, and one person can be shown dozens of times per round. The discount plus an impression cap spreads exposure at almost no cost in total friendships. Also watch yield decay round over round: the easy, real-world ties get used up, which is why a launch-week lift overstates the long-run effect.
